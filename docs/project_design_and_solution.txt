The proposed system, titled "EJBL: An Integrated Sales, Expense Tracking, Basic Bookkeeping, and FIFO Inventory Management System for EJBL Bigasan," is an internal-use mobile application designed to replace the store's manual sales notebook and fragmented spreadsheet workflow with a single unified platform. Built using Flutter and Dart for Android devices and web browsers as a Progressive Web App (PWA), and operating online through a centralized Supabase backend, the application serves only internal store personnel, while suppliers and business-to-business consignment clients are managed internally as database records rather than external system users.

The system is organized into four integrated modules: Log, Business Records, Inventory, and BIR Books with Capital. The Log module serves as the primary daily encoding interface, divided into Log In for sales transactions and Log Out for operational expenses. Sales entries support continuous official receipt (SI) numbering starting from 1 and auto-generated non-receipt (NSI) numbering for customers who decline a receipt, alongside multi-item orders in sacks or kilograms, tiered walk-in and delivery pricing, delivery fees recorded as store income, and cash, online, or split payments with reference numbers. Saving entries in the Log automatically populates Business Records to prevent duplicate encoding. Business Records consolidates the Cashbook—monitoring cash sales, online payments, cash box and GCash balances, overages or shortages, and consignment payment collections—together with the categorized Expenses ledger.

The Inventory module automates stock monitoring and First-In, First-Out (FIFO) costing by tracking each rice delivery's quantity and unit purchase cost (puhunan per kilogram), alongside an integrated rice variety code and price guide linked directly to the Log and low-stock ordering points. Recording an incoming rice restock automatically generates the corresponding Cost of Goods Sold expense entry. When a sale or business-to-business consignment delivery is logged, inventory is deducted immediately from the oldest available cost batch, whereas consignment payments—whether partial or full—are recorded in the Cashbook and Cash Receipt Journal only when payment is actually collected.

For basic bookkeeping, the BIR Books and Capital module automatically posts receipted (SI) sales, expenses, owner capital contributions, and personal withdrawals into four structured ledgers: the General Ledger, Cash Receipt Journal, Cash Disbursement Journal, and General Journal, while non-receipted (NSI) transactions remain tracked in Business Records and FIFO Inventory for owner and bookkeeper review or optional summary posting. The business owner can review, edit, or post manual journal entries and export the four BIR books to PDF. The exact column layouts of the four BIR books will be finalized and evaluated externally by a bookkeeper or Certified Public Accountant, who does not operate as an internal user of the application.

To protect operational and financial data, the system enforces role-based access control across three internal user levels. Employees or staff can add and save daily Log entries and view current inventory, but cannot override guide prices or access Business Records, Capital, or BIR Books. Managers can add, edit, save, and void or cancel Log records, manage inventory, override prices when authorized, and hold view-only access to Business Records. Every voided or cancelled transaction is preserved in a stored record rather than permanently deleted so that the business owner can audit all cancellations. The Admin or business owner holds full access across all modules, including Business Records, Capital, review of voided records, manual edits to the BIR Books, and PDF export of the BIR Books.
